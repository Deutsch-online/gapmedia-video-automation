# Critic report, 2026-10-05

## 2. Five biggest differences, ranked by impact on views
1. Frame 1 has no hook. Ref 1 opens on a furious grandma in a hat pointing at a tiny man, and you get the joke with no sound. Ours: a worried girl on a laptop (film), a smiling man with a coffee and four grammar chips (lesson), or a pose with no cause (Karim). Nothing is happening to anyone.
2. One action per scene. The refs show one sentence, shown by one visible action (stand up, jump, eat). Our lesson stacks 4 chips, a Persian explanation, a title bar and dots. Our film cuts between talking faces, so the picture never shows what the phrase means.
3. Caption. Refs: one short German line, huge, at the bottom, high contrast (red on translucent pill, or black/red on a yellow banner), with no translation. Ours: tiny German plus Persian under every line, gray and low-contrast on cream, and in tiles 6-7 the portrait covers the card ("Morge", "Nachmittag" clipped, a hidden heading). That is a visible layout bug.
4. Empty frame and UI chrome. In lesson tiles 4 and 5 about 40% of the frame is empty cream, and the cloud and clock icons are placeholders. The portrait is about a quarter of the frame width. The refs fill the frame with a character.
5. Character as the brand. Refs have the same two or one characters in every scene, with exaggerated reactions. Our film has 4+ faces that drift (Herr Pfeiffer changes face and clothes between tiles). Our Karim cast looks like one face repeated.

## 3. Measured gaps
- Duration: refs 64.3 s and 60.0 s, ours 55.7 s. That is not a real gap, but it is under the one-minute mark.
- Aspect: ref 1 is 1:1, so 9:16 is not what makes a short win. Ours is correctly 9:16 and 1080x1920, sharper than ref 2 (576x1024). Resolution is not our problem.
- Cut rate: refs 5-6.5 per minute (one 10 s take per scene). Ours reads 50.6, partly inflated by caption pops, but the sheet shows a new face about every 2.5 s. For a viewer that is a talking-face montage with no time to understand the joke.
- Silence share: refs 53-60%, ours 13.6%. Refs leave gaps so each sentence lands and repeats. Ours is wall-to-wall audio, so there is no beat for a laugh or for the viewer to repeat the phrase.
- Loudness: refs -23.1 LUFS (quiet), ours -17.0. Ours is louder and is not the problem. Do not copy the refs here.
- Frozen share: refs 0.2-1.9%, ours 2.5%. Equal. Ours has 3 black segments, which are a defect. Refs have 0-1.

## 4. Six highest-impact changes
1. Frame 1 must be a visible conflict that needs no sound: a character is doing something absurd, or being commanded or blamed, within 1 s. Karim stills 1, 4 and 6 (sunglasses grin, paper waving, hands on mouth) would do it. Drop the title bar, dots and chips from the start.
2. One German sentence per scene (6 total), and show its meaning as an action. No Persian text on screen during the scene. Put the Persian translation in the narration or in one closing card.
3. Caption: one line, at the bottom, at least 8% of frame height, solid or yellow banner behind it, a colored verb. It must not overlap the face or get clipped.
4. Fewer, longer shots. Hold each scene 8-10 s with the same two characters and a wide-then-close pair, as in ref 2. Cut the montage of faces.
5. Sound: leave gaps. Say the sentence, do the action, repeat the sentence, pause about 1 s. Aim for 40%+ silence share.
6. Lock the cast. Two characters with different silhouettes and colors (like pink hat vs blue cardigan). In Karim, give each office character a different color, hairstyle and body shape, and fix the black segments.
